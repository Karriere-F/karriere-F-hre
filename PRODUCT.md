# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three distinct audiences on one site:

- **Candidates** in Cameroon and Central Africa (primarily French-speaking, likely
  majority-mobile) considering a career or training move to Germany — some already at
  German B2 ("Fast Track"), others starting from scratch and needing the full A1→B2
  training path ("Full Training"). They are making a high-stakes, often first-time
  decision about their career and possibly relocating internationally.
- **German employers** facing skilled-labor shortages (Fachkräftemangel), evaluating
  whether this agency is a credible, vetted source of workforce.
- **Karriere Fähre's own team**, using the admin dashboard to validate employers,
  manage the candidate pool, and edit site content — a small, secondary "product"
  surface behind the marketing site.

## Product Purpose

Karriere Fähre is a staffing/recruitment and German-language-training agency (SARL,
Douala, Cameroon) that places candidates with German companies short on skilled labor,
training those who aren't yet at B2 German first. The website's job is to build enough
trust with both candidates and employers that they take the first step (sign up, submit
a staffing need, book a call) on what is, for most visitors, a life-changing decision.
Success looks like qualified candidates and legitimate employers both choosing to
engage, in a market (Central Africa ↔ Germany placement) where trust is the primary
barrier to conversion, not awareness.

## Brand Personality

Reassuring, professional, human. This is not a growth-hacked SaaS product — it's
recruitment and immigration-adjacent, so visitors are evaluating credibility and safety
before anything else. Founder credibility (dual German-Cameroonian background) is a
core trust asset, not a footnote. Confidence without hype; warmth without informality.

## Anti-references

No specific named anti-references from the founder — default to avoiding the generic
"AI SaaS" register entirely (gradient text, eyebrow labels on every section, identical
icon-card grids, cream-default body backgrounds chosen by default rather than intent).
The existing black/gold/cream brand identity (from the provided logo) should anchor
every decision instead of a generic startup palette.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust over hype.** Every design decision should lower anxiety around a
   life-changing decision (relocating, changing career), not manufacture urgency or
   excitement. No pricing on the public site; process transparency instead of sales
   pressure.
2. **Two audiences, never crossed.** Candidates and employers have different jobs to be
   done. Never make one wade through content meant for the other; keep their paths
   (signup, dashboard, CTAs) clearly separated even when they share a page.
3. **Credible in three languages equally.** The trilingual site (FR/DE/EN) is itself a
   trust signal — French for the primary candidate audience, German for employer
   credibility, English for reach. Typography, spacing, and layout must hold up
   identically in all three, not just the language it was designed in.
4. **Mobile-first for the primary audience.** Most candidates are on mobile. Every
   layout, form, and interaction is designed at mobile width first, not adapted down
   from desktop.
5. **Show the process, don't oversell it.** Let concrete steps (test → training or
   direct placement → matching → integration support) carry credibility rather than
   marketing copy or manufactured social proof.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA baseline (contrast ratios, focus states, semantic structure) — no
additional constraints specified by the founder. `prefers-reduced-motion` is already
respected across the site's motion system (see `src/app/globals.css`).
